Your Daily Computer Brief

🔴 ALERT

A text message arrives: "Your package couldn't be delivered. Click here to reschedule: [link]." Or an email: "USPS/UPS/FedEx delivery failed — confirm your address." The link goes to a fake login page that steals your email password or credit card. This wave is hitting hard right now because holiday shopping season means more real packages — and crooks know you're expecting deliveries. What to do: Don't click the link. If you're expecting a package, go to the carrier's real website (type usps.com, ups.com, or fedex.com yourself) and enter the tracking number there. Or call the number on your receipt. Real carriers never ask for your email password or credit card to "reschedule." I've had two customers this week who clicked one of these on their phone, then logged in on their computer — and the crooks had their email password within minutes. One lost access to their banking because the same password was reused. Source: FTC Consumer Alert — "Fake shipping notifications" (October 2026); AARP Fraud Watch Network — "Package delivery scams surge ahead of holidays" (October 2026); Malwarebytes Labs — "Smishing kits targeting USPS/UPS/FedEx" (October 2026)
