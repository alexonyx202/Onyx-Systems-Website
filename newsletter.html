Your Daily Computer Brief

🔴 ALERT

Scammers Are Faking Official-Looking Notices — Here's the Tell. You get an email or see a webpage that looks exactly like it's from your bank, the IRS, or a government agency. The logo is perfect. The formatting is perfect. It says "Immediate action required" and threatens account closure, legal trouble, or lost benefits if you don't respond today. The giveaway: they ask for something no legitimate organization would ever request by email. Your Social Security number. Your bank login. A payment in gift cards or wire transfer. Remote access to your computer. Real banks and government agencies don't operate this way. They already have your information. They don't threaten instant disaster. They don't demand gift cards. What to do: Don't click any links in the message. Don't call any phone number in it. Look up the real organization's contact info yourself (from a statement or official website) and contact them directly. Delete the suspicious message. Source: FTC.gov Consumer Alerts — fake official fraud remains the most reported fraud type for the ninth straight year.
