Your Daily Computer Brief

🔴 ALERT

Security writers are warning about fake browser-update pop-ups: a notice claiming your browser needs a "critical update." Clicking it installs malware, not an update. Real updates come from the browser's own menu — never from a website pop-up. If you see one, close the tab without clicking, then update properly: in Chrome, click the three dots, choose **Help → About Google Chrome**, and let it update itself. Then check your extensions and remove anything you don't recognize. *Source: Android Authority — August 13, 2026; Lifehacker — August 2026*

Today in Tech

  • **Fake update pop-ups spread malware:** A compromised browser extension can trigger convincing fake "update required" notices that download malicious scripts. *Source: Android Authority — August 13, 2026.*
  • **Malicious extensions caught:** Security researchers found over 300 Chrome extensions leaking browser data or stealing user information. *Source: SecurityWeek — February 14, 2026.*
  • **Tech support scams persist:** The FTC warns that fake pop-ups about "problems with your computer" are a common scam opening. *Source: FTC Consumer Advice.*

Daily Tip

Extensions are the usual way this scam gets in. In Chrome, click the three dots, choose **Extensions → Manage Extensions**, and turn off anything you don't recognize or no longer use — one at a time, so you'll know what changed. Two minutes, and it closes the door this scam walks through.
