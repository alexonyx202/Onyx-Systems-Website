🔴 Alert

Fake "Apple Support" FaceTime Calls Empty Bank Accounts

Apple is warning that scammers are using FaceTime calls — not just texts — to pose as "Apple Support" or your bank. A live video or audio call feels more legitimate, so people let their guard down and hand over card details, banking logins, or one-time passcodes. Apple and your bank will NOT FaceTime you about fraud. Hang up, then call the number on the back of your card. Report fake calls to reportfacetimefraud@apple.com. Source: Malwarebytes, July 14, 2026

💡 Daily Tip

Freeze Your Credit (It's Free)

After every breach, the real damage is someone opening new accounts in your name. A credit freeze blocks that — and it's free. Visit each bureau (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion), create a login, and toggle the freeze on. It takes about five minutes total and doesn't hurt your score. When you actually need credit, thaw it temporarily, then re-freeze. Simple, free, and it stops new-account fraud cold.
