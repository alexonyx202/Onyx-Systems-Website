🔴 ALERT

That "Install Browser Update" Page Is a Trap. You visit a website and suddenly a page appears that looks exactly like a Chrome or Firefox notice. It says your browser is out of date and you need to install the latest version right now. There's a button that says "Install" or "Download." Here's the truth: real browser updates never happen through a random webpage. Chrome, Firefox, and Edge update themselves automatically in the background, or you check for them from the browser's own menu (three dots → Help → About). A webpage cannot install software on your computer. What to do: Close the tab immediately. Do not click any button on that page. If you're unsure, open your browser's menu and look for "About" — it will show your version and update status. Takes two seconds. Scammers use these fake pages to trick you into downloading a program that steals passwords and takes control of your computer. A new Chrome vulnerability (CVE-2026-125350) was added to the government's actively-exploited list on November 10, making these scams even more dangerous right now. Source: CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog — November 10, 2026 Source: FTC Consumer Alerts — "Fake Browser Update Scams"

Daily Tip

Deleted files sit in the Recycle Bin taking up space until you empty it. Right-click the Recycle Bin icon on your desktop and choose "Empty Recycle Bin." This frees up that space immediately. If your computer has been slow, this simple step can recover several gigabytes in seconds.
