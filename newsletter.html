Your Daily Computer Brief

— CISA Warns: Hackers Are Actively Exploiting On-Prem SharePoint

CISA sounded the alarm on July 14 after attackers began actively exploiting three flaws in on-premises Microsoft SharePoint Server — including CVE-2026-56164, freshly added to its exploited-vulnerabilities catalog (source: CISA.gov, reported by The Register, July 15). A successful hit lets intruders run code and steal data from any business, school, or church running SharePoint in-house. If your organization hosts SharePoint on its own servers, apply Microsoft's patches and rotate machine keys today. If you can't patch immediately, shut down outside access to the server until you can — don't wait for the next breach headline.

Today in Tech

  • Microsoft paused its July 2026 Windows 11 update (KB5101650) for some Dell PCs after a bad Intel driver caused shutdowns, overheating, and battery drain (source: WindowsReport, July 15). If your Dell hasn't offered the patch yet, that's by design — sit tight.
  • Mozilla and Google shipped critical browser fixes this week: Firefox patched two flaws with public exploit code, and Chrome's July 14 stable update closed its own holes (source: The Hacker News, July 15). Update your browser now — one click in Settings.
  • A new LLM-assisted IoT botnet, TuxBot v3, is hijacking poorly secured smart devices to build DDoS networks (source: The Hacker News, 17 hours ago). Change every default password on your cameras, routers, and smart gadgets today.

Daily Tip

Reusing one password everywhere means a single leaked login unlocks your whole life. Grab a free password manager (Bitwarden is solid), open the app, and click "generate" so it makes one long random password per site. Turn on two-factor authentication where you can. It takes ten minutes to set up and saves you from the nightmare of a wiped-out account.
